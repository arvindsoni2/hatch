# STAR Framework for Behavioural Interview Answers

## What STAR means

| Letter | Stands for | Guidance |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **S** | Situation | Set the scene in 1–2 sentences. Context only — no action yet. |
| **T** | Task | What was your specific responsibility or goal? |
| **A** | Action | What did YOU do? Use "I", not "we". Be specific. |
| **R** | Result | Quantify the outcome. What changed because of your action? |

## Target length

- **Spoken**: 90 seconds (≈ 200 words).
- **Written model answer**: ≤ 200 words.

## Common mistakes

1. **Spending too long on S/T, rushing R** — allocate 10% / 10% / 60% / 20%.
2. **Using "we" throughout the Action section** — interviewers are assessing your contribution, not your team's.
3. **Vague results** — "improved performance" vs "reduced mean cycle time from 14 to 6 days".
4. **Situation too distant** — prefer examples from the last 5 years unless the role specifically asks for formative experience.
5. **Missing the "so what"** — always close with the business impact, not just the technical outcome.

## Example (Delivery Lead)

**Question:** Tell me about a time you turned around a failing project.

**S:** In 2023, I inherited a £4M digital transformation at Northern Powergrid that was 6 weeks behind schedule and had lost the confidence of its executive sponsor.

**T:** My task was to recover the programme and restore stakeholder trust within one quarter.

**A:** I ran a structured root-cause session with the delivery team, identified three dependency blockers, negotiated a 2-week scope reduction with the business, and introduced weekly exec steering with written RAG status reports.

**R:** We shipped on the revised date, received formal sign-off from the sponsor, and the programme's mobile platform generated £500K in annual savings — which became a proof point in the following year's budget bid.
